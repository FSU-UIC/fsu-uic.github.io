Approach

One problem, five layers

Ubiquitous intelligence breaks whenever a layer is treated in isolation: a waveform that ignores the radio front end, a sensing pipeline that ignores the network, a model that ignores the physics of the signal it consumes. My work follows a single vertical cut through the stack, which we call XL-Stack. Each layer is a research contribution in its own right, and each one is designed so the layer above it can stand on it. The sections below walk the stack from the bottom up.

  1. L5 Intelligence & Physical AI Foundation models that understand and generate physical motion
  2. L4 Integrated Sensing & Communication The same radio both carries data and perceives the world
  3. L3 Cross-Technology Networking & Spectrum Heterogeneous radios sharing one crowded band
  4. L2 mmWave Links & Beam Management Directional links that find and hold each other in milliseconds
  5. L1 Signals, Waveforms & Battery-Free Hardware Bits carried by ambient energy and reshaped waveforms

Cross-cutting Security & privacy is not a sixth layer. Every layer leaks, and every layer can be attacked, so it is studied inside all five.

L1

Signals, Waveforms & Battery-Free Hardware

At the bottom of the stack the question is how little power a device needs to say something. Backscatter answers it by reflecting the signals already in the air rather than generating new ones — which means the physical layer of an existing standard has to be reverse-engineered and then rebuilt on a device with almost no energy budget.

L3

Cross-Technology Networking & Spectrum

Wi-Fi, ZigBee, Bluetooth and LTE share the same air but were never designed to talk to each other. This layer makes incompatible radios interoperate directly — without a gateway, without new hardware — and studies what happens to the spectrum when they do.

L4

Integrated Sensing & Communication

Once a radio is understood well enough to be rebuilt, it can be asked to do a second job: perceive. This layer extracts respiration, gesture, pose and identity from the same commodity signals that are already carrying data — and asks what a person's body involuntarily reveals to any receiver in the room.

L5

Intelligence & Physical AI

The top of the stack is where measurements become understanding. We build models that read human motion from wireless and visual signals, generate it, and run inside the tight budget of an edge device — grounded in the geometry and physics of the layers underneath rather than treating the signal as an opaque tensor.

Cross-Cutting: Security & Privacy

Every technique that lets a system perceive more also lets an adversary perceive more. This thread runs vertically through the stack: side channels in optical and RF links, attacks on autonomous vehicles through invisible light, jamming across incompatible radios, and defenses built at the layer where the leak actually happens.

Background

Before FSU

I was a postdoctoral scholar at the NSF AI-EDGE Institute at The Ohio State University, working with Prof. Ness Shroff, Prof. Eylem Ekici and Prof. Kannan Srinivasan on bringing machine learning into next-generation wireless networks. I received my Ph.D. from the University of Maryland, Baltimore County, advised by Prof. Ting Zhu, on wireless communication, networking, sensing and security. Before academia I spent years building high-precision measurement instruments end to end — high-speed and high-power circuits, MCU/FPGA/DSP/ARM firmware, sensor integration, laser measurement and projection, motor and mechanical control — which is where the habit of reasoning across the whole stack comes from.

I am always glad to hear from students and collaborators, especially those working at the seam between two of these layers.
